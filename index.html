HALACHA

…why we wait six hours between meat and milk?

Kashrus · Week of 3 November 2026
This week’s paper is sponsored l’iluy nishmas [Name] · Sponsor the next one

Most of us learned the rule as small children: after a fleishig meal, we wait six hours before eating anything milchig. Yet the Torah says nothing of a wait, and the Gemara records only the practice of Mar Ukva, who waited “from one meal to the next.”1 How did that become six hours — and why do some families wait one, or three?

Two reasons, two approaches

Rashi understands the wait as a measure of time: the span that separated the morning and evening meals of the Talmudic era.2 The Rambam offers a physical reason — meat lodged between the teeth retains its taste for roughly six hours.3 The Shulchan Aruch rules six hours, citing both explanations, and this is the prevalent practice today.4

Q: If I only chewed meat for a baby, and didn’t swallow, must I wait?

Here the two reasons diverge. According to the Rambam, meat in the mouth is the concern, so chewing alone would require a wait; according to Rashi, one who did not eat a meal has nothing to wait for.5 The Shach rules stringently, in deference to both.6

Bottom line
  • Wait six hours after meat before dairy.
  • Chewing without swallowing — also wait.
  • Follow an established family minhag of one or three hours.
  • Ask your rav about children and illness.

Where the one-hour minhag comes from

The Rema records a custom to wait one hour, recite a berachah acharonah, and clean the mouth — a practice preserved in many Dutch and German communities.7 Those who keep it are following a legitimate opinion, not a leniency…

2Rashi, ibid., s.v. l’seudasa.

3Rambam, Hil. Ma’achalos Asuros 9:28.

4Shulchan Aruch, Y.D. 89:1.

5See Pri Megadim, Sifsei Da’as 89:1.

6Shach, Y.D. 89:2.

7Rema, ibid.; Darchei Moshe 89:2.
